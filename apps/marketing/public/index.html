  1. 01 · You

    Write the brief

    Say what you need, in plain words. “A calm launch teaser, no gradients” is plenty. Vietnamese works too.

  2. 02 · The director

    Set the taste

    A top model (Claude Opus) decides the story, pacing, type and color. It is expensive, so it is only called when taste matters.

    It does not write code. It has people for that.

  3. 03 · The crew

    Build every scene

    Code and small, fast models turn the plan into scenes. A producer (Jev) decides who fixes what, and whether a fix is worth paying for.

  4. 04 · The editor

    Check, then render

    Every scene is checked frame by frame before the final render. You get a web-ready MP4 and a link to share it.

Fair questions.

Do I need to know how to code or edit video?

No. You describe the video in plain words, to your AI assistant or in the dashboard. The code and the editing happen behind the scenes.

What kinds of videos can it make?

Motion graphics: launch teasers, explainers, social reels, data stories, invitations and announcements, in 16:9, 9:16, 1:1 or 4:5. It composes text, shapes, numbers and code on screen. It does not film people or generate photoreal footage.

What if I don't like the result?

Say what to change: “bigger date”, “slower second scene”, “warmer colors”. Only the affected scenes are rebuilt, so the parts you liked stay put.

Can I download and post the videos?

Yes. Every final render is a web-ready MP4 you can download, or share through a stable link from the dashboard.

Can I run it on my own servers?

Yes. The whole stack is open source under the MIT licence. Bring your own model keys, and billing can be switched off entirely.
